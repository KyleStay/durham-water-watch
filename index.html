Restriction stages and triggers

These rules cover City water. Each stage keeps earlier restrictions unless a stricter rule replaces them. Year-round efficiency rules still apply outside a declared shortage.

The triggers below use the City model’s probability of reaching a combined usable reservoir storage level at the end of a forecast period. They are not today’s storage or days-of-supply cutoffs.

Stage 1

Model trigger: 30% chance of 45% usable storage in 12 weeks.

  • Spray irrigation: one assigned day a week, outside 10 a.m.–6 p.m. Hand and drip watering remain allowed.
  • Restaurants serve water on request. Decorative water must recycle; swimming pools may only replace losses and maintain chemical equipment. No new landscape exemptions.

Stage 2 · In effect

Model trigger: 20% chance of 35% usable storage in 10 weeks.

  • Landscape spray irrigation stops. Hand watering, drip irrigation and watering bags remain allowed.
  • Vehicle washing only at compliant commercial or institutional facilities. Surface washing only before painting or for health or safety.

Stage 3

Model trigger: 10% chance of 25% usable storage in 8 weeks.

  • Hand and drip landscape watering also stop. Irrigation is limited to container plants and commercial plants for sale; other outdoor use is limited to public health and safety.
  • Compliant commercial or institutional car washes may operate. Reduce nonessential heating and cooling water use as practical.

Stage 4 · Emergency

Model trigger: 5% chance of 15% usable storage in 4 weeks.

  • Water emergency: outdoor use only for public health and safety. Vehicle washing stops; no water added to decorative features or pools, except prescribed medical or rehabilitation pool use.
  • Other uses are limited to minimum health and safety needs. The City may reduce or discontinue service to preserve essential supplies.
Exceptions and how stages change

The City Manager declares stage changes and can act before a trigger, skip stages, or delay lifting restrictions, considering forecasts, demand, neighboring supplies, water quality and system emergencies. This dashboard does not run the City’s model or predict the next stage.

The plan’s rescission criterion is a 95% probability of reaching 95% combined usable storage in 10 weeks, with reservoir volumes increasing. Restrictions end only through an official City decision.

Stages 1–2 include limited irrigation exemptions and approved conservation plans. Users above 100,000 gallons/day must attempt and document reductions of 15%, 30% and 50% in Stages 1, 2 and 3, respectively. Construction restrictions require separate City action; consult the complete rules for exemptions and variances.

Stage 2 water-use rules

Plain-language summary of the currently verified City page. The complete official rules control.

×Do not

  • No landscape spray irrigation with City water, including hose-end sprinklers.
  • No vehicle washing, except at a commercial or institutional car wash.
  • No washing sidewalks, driveways, decks, exterior walls, or paved areas.
  • Do not add water to decorative fountains, ponds, or pools unless recycled.

✓Allowed alternatives

  • Hand watering, drip irrigation, and tree or shrub watering bags.
  • Watering container plants and commercial plant stock for sale.
  • Surface washing for a health or safety issue, or before painting.
  • Pool water only for evaporation or spillage loss and chemical feed operation.

iOther Stage 2 rules

  • Restaurants serve drinking water only when requested.
  • Users above 100,000 gallons/day should target a 30% reduction and document efforts.
  • Approved Water Conservation Plans and limited exemptions may apply.
  • Violations may lead to civil penalties or service termination.

Why these can differ

Drought describes regional dryness. Durham’s stage also reflects usable storage, demand, treatment and distribution conditions, forecasts, and management decisions. One does not mechanically set the other.

Drought categories: D0–D4

Experts assess rainfall, soil moisture, streamflow, groundwater and reported impacts together each week. The percentile bands are typical indicator ranges, not automatic triggers: the 10th percentile means conditions are as dry as or drier than about 90% of the historical record.

Drought severity, typical indicator percentiles and possible impacts
CategoryTypical percentile (approx.)What it means
None · Normal or wet>30No drought classification.
D0 · Abnormally dry20–30Dryness developing or lingering after drought; not itself drought.
D1 · Moderate drought10–20Plant stress and declining streams or reservoirs may develop.
D2 · Severe drought5–10Crop losses and water shortages can intensify.
D3 · Extreme drought2–5More serious water shortages and widespread impacts are possible.
D4 · Exceptional drought0–2Critical shortages can affect communities, agriculture and daily life.

NC assigns each county the highest category covering at least 25% of its land area. These categories describe conditions; they do not themselves impose Durham’s water-use rules or correspond one-to-one with City stages.

Stage 2 exit outlook

How close is Durham to leaving Stage 2?

?Durham’s forecast determines when it can lift restrictions.

The City can lift Stage 2 when reservoir volumes are rising and its forecast meets the probability test below.

The plan’s rescission test

≥95%modeled probability
→
95%combined usable storage
→
10week forecast horizon

Meeting the modeled test does not automatically end Stage 2. The plan allows the City Manager to delay rescission or consider other system conditions.

Read the official response plan, Tables 5.1–5.2 ↗

How the City’s forecast is described

  1. Model usable storage.The plan uses combined usable reservoir storage—not individual elevation percentages—as the key forecast input.
  2. Project demand and system conditions.The City considers demand, lake elevations, intake access, treatment and distribution capacity, neighboring supplies, weather, and other operational factors.
  3. Test the 10-week probability.The model estimates the chance of reaching the combined-storage target by the end of the forecast horizon.
  4. Apply management judgment.The City may change stages non-sequentially, act earlier, or rescind more slowly as conditions warrant.

What does not measure closeness

  • Estimated days of supply is not an exit threshold.
  • The NC drought category does not mechanically set the City stage.
  • Feet below full cannot be converted to percent storage without official storage curves.
  • Streamflow cannot predict reservoir refill or a stage change.

If Durham publishes the modeled probability and combined-storage forecast, this dashboard can show that official outlook directly and compare it with the plan—without inventing a forecast.

Sources & methodology

Trust is a product feature.

This dashboard links every operational metric to its authoritative source, keeps the last verified value when refreshes fail, and never substitutes an estimate.

01

Meaning, not mystery

Days of supply combines accessible reservoir water, less-accessible water below the intakes, and Teer Quarry emergency storage. Elevation is never presented as percent storage.

02

Refresh rhythm

A daily publication checks Durham, USGS, and NC drought sources at about 6 AM Eastern. Source observation dates can be older than the publication. Freshness labels age in your browser; the readings change at the next publication.

03

Stale thresholds

Durham daily metrics: two calendar days. USGS: about three hours. NC drought: after the expected weekly update window.

04

Validation before publication

Expected URL and units, a recognizable date, nonnegative components, internally consistent totals, intended reservoir fields, newer observations, and plausible changes.

05

Failure is visible

Malformed, older, null, or implausible readings never replace the repository’s last-known-good snapshot. The next static build keeps the verified value with a stale label; without one, it says reading unavailable.

06

Limits

City chart images are not digitized. Streamflow is not used to estimate storage or refill. Trend claims require at least two locally verified readings.
